FOLIO 02.1 · CAGE 铁笼与生机

方寸之地，挤着八九条鲜活生命。它们化作交易里的筹码，我的心，同那铁笼一同颤栗。

A space mere feet across, packed with eight or nine vivid lives. They became chips in a trade, and my heart shuddered along with that iron cage.

FIG 02 · APPLE 1 / 5
FOLIO 02.2 · APPLE STAYED 五取其一

那时我尚年少，不忍断它们生机。一窝添了五个小小的生灵，疫病袭来，我拼力护住四只，一只远去，“苹果”留了下来。

I was still young then, and could not bear to end their line. The nest gained five tiny lives, then sickness struck — I fought with all I had to keep four alive, one slipped away; "Apple" stayed.

FOLIO 02.3 · MEANING OF SAVING

它命途多舛，疫病掠去后腿气力，幸而留住性命；其余的，被母猫携走。我向父母求助，他们百般为难。我眼睁睁看着生命在眼前流走，那一刻，才懂得积蓄的意义。

Her fate was rough — the illness took the strength of her back legs, yet spared her life; the rest were carried off by the mother cat. I turned to my parents for help; they put up every difficulty. I watched lives slip away before my own eyes, and in that moment, I learned what saving means.

LOG 03.1 · FIRST BREATH 烟波与航道

2025 年盛夏，我来到洞庭湖。湖面辽阔，风声很远，我却第一次听见江豚的呼吸。它们在烟波里忽隐忽现，不似传说，也不似梦境，只是一群真实而脆弱的生命。后来我才知道，湖里有江豚，也有渔船，有水位与航道的变化，有保护，也有冲突。

In midsummer 2025, I came to Dongting Lake. The lake spread wide, the wind sounded far away, and for the first time, I heard a porpoise breathe. They flickered in and out of the mist — just a group of real, fragile lives.

LOG 03.2 · GUARDING 日复一日地靠近

一个小小的渡口，装不下所有答案。
但我开始懂得：守护不只是看见，更是日复一日地靠近。

Guarding is more than seeing — it is drawing near, day after day.

我仍怕惊扰它们，也怕自己走得太慢。可有些路，必须一步步走去，有些爱，也必须在坚持里，慢慢长成力量。

04 — 星期五 / FRIDAYS

Every Friday. Bit by bit.

每到周五，零钱悄悄扣去一点，无声无息，翻过一个又一个星期。

Every Friday, a little slips quietly out of my wallet, soundless, turning over one week after another.

读巴菲特的信，最见耐心是等候。我也在等，等时光推着我慢慢成熟。

In Buffett's letters, patience shows most in the waiting. I am waiting too — letting time push me slowly toward maturity.

FOLIO 05 · TOTE BAG & STARLIGHT 90 MINUTES

志愿相伴的那个孩子，在布袋上画《星月夜》。落笔时他慌了，怕画不完漫天星辰。

我说，先点亮夜空第一颗星，再牵起盘旋翻涌的长线。不必强求梵高的热烈，那只袋子，连同他眼里的星光，我好好留到今天。

The child I kept company with as a volunteer was painting The Starry Night on a tote bag. At the first stroke he panicked, afraid he could never finish a sky full of stars.

I said: light the first star into the night sky first, then take up the long, swirling, churning line. No need to force Van Gogh's intensity — that bag, together with the starlight in his eyes, I have kept carefully to this day.
